# Disagreeing on our app release testing approach
## Solvex Pay - Senior Software Engineer, Money Movement Team

### Context
Our team had different priorities for post-release testing — the product owner capped it at an hour, some teammates spent 30 minutes, others an hour, and everyone tested different things depending on mood and memory. Whoever got randomly assigned mid-sprint would drop what they were doing, which kept disrupting delivery. I disagreed with the approach but got no traction the first time I raised it.

### Stakeholders
- Product Owner (set the time budget)
- Money Movement Team (performed the testing)

### Table of content
Raised a concern about an inconsistent, randomly-assigned release testing process, got no traction the first time, raised it again with concrete examples of the inconsistency, and got the team to align on a clear scope and a round-robin assignment.

### Actions
1. Raised the inconsistency once in a retro, framed generally — it didn't land, and the team kept the status quo.
2. Collected concrete examples over the next two releases of what different people tested (or missed) under the same random-assignment process.
3. Raised it a second time with those specific examples, showing the pattern rather than a vague complaint.
4. Proposed two concrete changes: a written testing scope everyone would follow, and a round-robin assignment instead of random picks.
5. Facilitated the discussion to a decision in the same retro, rather than letting it become another unresolved agenda item.

### Result and impact
- The team aligned on a clear, written testing scope and switched to round-robin assignment.
- No one was blindsided mid-sprint by a random assignment again.
- Post-release regressions caught by manual testing became more consistent release over release.

### Learnings
- A vague complaint rarely moves a team; concrete, collected examples of the actual inconsistency make the problem impossible to wave away.
- Facilitating toward a decision in the same conversation matters as much as raising the issue — otherwise it becomes another unresolved agenda item.

### Signal areas
- **Conflict resolutions**: Raised a disagreement twice, the second time with data, until it landed.
- **Ownership**: Didn't just complain — proposed and drove the concrete process change.
- **Communication**: Used specific examples instead of general complaints to make the case land.
