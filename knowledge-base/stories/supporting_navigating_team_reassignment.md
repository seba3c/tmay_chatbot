# Team reassignment
## Solvex Pay - Senior Software Engineer, Money Movement Team

### Context
I had been on the Money Movement Team for almost a year, and I had just reached a point where I felt confident and owned the team's services and flows. That was when I was told my role was being reassigned to a new team because of a company reorganization. I disagreed with the decision at first — I felt confident enough to keep delivering more value and having a bigger impact on my current team. I was also upset that no one had asked for my opinion or preferences.

### Stakeholders
- Money Movement Team (current team)
- Risk Team (new team)

### Table of content
Disagreed with a reassignment decision but listened to the reasons, committed to the move, planned a soft transition by closing and handing off my work in progress on the Money Movement Team, then onboarded quickly to the Risk Team and started delivering.

### Actions
1. I expressed my point of view and preferences to my direct leader.
2. I listened carefully to the reasons behind the decision — mainly that, because of my previous experience on a similar team, I was seen as the best fit at that moment.
3. I understood the situation and decided to disagree and commit.
4. I took some extra time on my current team to close out work in progress. I was in the middle of a big feature we had just released, so I made sure to properly close it and transfer all the context to my teammates so they could take it over from there.
5. I joined the new team and, from day one, I did my best to onboard quickly and start delivering as soon as possible.

### Result and impact
- I quickly increased the capacity of my new team — within about two weeks I was already delivering features end to end on my own, and we were able to take on more work each sprint.
- I planned a soft transition and made sure all my work on the Money Movement Team was in good hands before I moved, so the team had no major issues picking up my initiatives.

### Learnings
- Sometimes you can disagree with a decision but still be professional enough to commit and push it through.
- When you move to a new team or leave a company, a soft transition matters: properly transferring tasks and knowledge to your teammates makes their life easier in the short term.

### Signal areas
- **Conflict resolutions**: I disagreed with the reassignment but engaged constructively — I expressed my view, listened to the reasons, and then committed and pushed the decision forward rather than resisting it.
- **Ownership**: I took responsibility for a smooth handoff on my current team — closing work in progress and transferring context — and for ramping up fast on the new team to deliver quickly.
- **Communication**: I shared my preferences openly with my leader, listened to the reasoning behind the decision, and made sure my teammates had all the information they needed to take over my work.
- **Growth**: I took concrete lessons from the experience — committing professionally even when I disagreed, and treating transitions as knowledge-transfer moments — and applied them to later team changes.
