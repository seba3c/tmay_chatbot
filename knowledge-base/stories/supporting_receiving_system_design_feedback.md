# Receive feedback: wrong wording when discussing system design and architectural decisions
## Solvex Pay - Senior Software Engineer, Money Movement Team

### Context
My manager pointed out that my blunt, declarative style in design meetings was hurting buy-in instead of building it. I'd been making declarative statements in architecture discussions ("this solution is just better") that were shutting down discussion and generating friction instead of collaboration.

### Stakeholders
- Manager (gave the feedback)
- Money Movement Team (participants in design discussions)

### Table of content
Received feedback that a declarative communication style was hurting buy-in in design meetings, and replaced it with data-backed, inquiry-based questions — improving both the quality of the discussions and my own influence over the outcomes.

### Actions
1. Took the feedback at face value rather than getting defensive, and asked for a specific recent example so I could see it concretely.
2. Consciously replaced declarative statements ("this is just better") with questions ("what do you think about this trade-off?") in the next few design meetings.
3. Started showing up with data and benchmarks instead of opinions, so the conversation moved from "who's right" to "what do the facts say."
4. Deliberately listened more than I talked in the first half of each design session, before offering my own view.

### Result and impact
- Design discussions became noticeably more productive, with more people participating instead of going quiet.
- I ended up influencing the final architectural decisions more than before, not less.
- Even when the team went in a different direction than I'd proposed, I found it much easier to disagree and commit, because the process felt fair to everyone.

### Learnings
- Declarative statements shut down discussion even when you're right; questions keep people engaged and surface the real objections.
- Leading with data instead of opinion is the fastest way to resolve a technical disagreement without it becoming personal.

### Signal areas
- **Growth**: Took direct feedback about communication style and visibly changed the behavior.
- **Communication**: Shifted from declarative statements to inquiry and data, improving both discussion quality and personal influence.
