# Cross-team collaborations: cloud migration, LogFerry, and ShieldScan
## Solvex Pay - Senior Software Engineer, Money Movement Team

### Context
Over one quarter, my team was a stakeholder in three cross-team initiatives at once: an AWS account migration led by DevOps, a monitoring swap to LogFerry led by the Enabling team, and a ShieldScan security-scanner rollout led by Security. I ended up being my team's point of contact for all three.

### Stakeholders
- DevOps Team (AWS account migration)
- Enabling Team (LogFerry monitoring swap)
- Security Team (ShieldScan rollout)
- Money Movement Team (my team, affected by all three)

### Table of content
Acted as the single point of contact for three simultaneous cross-team initiatives, reading each owning team's documentation before touching anything, staying in their sync cadence, and following through with post-launch monitoring and feedback for all three.

### Actions
1. For each initiative, I started by reading the owning team's documentation and reference setup before making any changes on our side.
2. Joined the Security, Enabling, and DevOps syncs so I had near-real-time visibility into changes that could affect us.
3. Confirmed staging and production status with each team before we deployed our side of each migration.
4. Coordinated the sequencing so the three migrations didn't collide — for example, delaying our LogFerry cutover by a week to avoid overlapping with the AWS account migration window.
5. After each go-live, I watched our services for several days and reported back to the owning team with any issues or confirmation of a clean migration.

### Result and impact
- All three migrations landed on our team with zero customer-facing incidents.
- The follow-through and reporting earned positive feedback from all three owning teams, which made the next round of cross-team initiatives faster because the trust was already established.
- The sequencing coordination I proposed was adopted as a lightweight template for future company-wide rollouts.

### Learnings
- Understanding the owning team's world before making any request builds credibility fast, and it's worth the upfront time.
- Being the single accountable bridge across several parallel initiatives prevents things from falling through the cracks, especially when the initiatives can interact with each other.
- Following through after go-live, not just during it, is what turns a one-off collaboration into long-term trust.

### Signal areas
- **Communication**: Stayed in sync with three different teams simultaneously and reported back consistently.
- **Ownership**: Took responsibility for how three external changes landed on my own team, not just for my own team's tasks.
- **Scope**: Coordinated sequencing across initiatives that individually belonged to other teams.
