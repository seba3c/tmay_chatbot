# Receive positive feedback on a mid-size PR
## Solvex Pay - Senior Software Engineer, Money Movement Team

### Context
I shipped a mid-size PR for our new corporate transfer flow — a feature with a fair amount of surface area but not a huge, headline project. My tech leader reviewed it and messaged me afterward specifically to call out the quality of the work.

### Stakeholders
- Tech Leader (gave the feedback)
- Money Movement Team (benefited from the norm shift)

### Table of content
Received specific, positive feedback on a mid-size PR from a tech leader, which reinforced a review habit that I then carried into how I review my own teammates' work.

### Actions
1. Shipped the PR with the same rigor I'd apply to a larger feature — clear commits, tests for the risky paths, and a description explaining the tradeoffs I'd made.
2. My tech leader messaged me directly, rather than just approving silently, to say that keeping that quality on a PR of that size is hard to achieve.
3. I asked what specifically stood out, to understand what to keep doing rather than assuming.
4. I started deliberately leaving similar, specific positive comments on my teammates' PRs, not just flagging defects.

### Result and impact
- The small, specific piece of praise boosted my confidence and stuck with me well beyond the PR itself.
- I've since made "reinforce the good, not just the gaps" part of how I review code, and a few teammates have mentioned it changed how they see code review.

### Learnings
- Specific praise ("this part, for this reason") lands much better than generic praise ("nice work") — it tells you exactly what to keep doing.
- Positive feedback is a review habit worth practicing deliberately, not just a nice-to-have — it shapes team culture as much as catching bugs does.

### Signal areas
- **Growth**: Internalized a lasting review habit from a single piece of feedback.
- **Communication**: Asked a clarifying follow-up to understand the feedback, then paid the habit forward to teammates.
